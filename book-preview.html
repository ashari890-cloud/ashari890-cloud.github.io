Trauma Didn’t Win:
Born to Break Cycles

By Ashari Allen

This preview contains the Author’s Note and Prologue only. The complete manuscript is not published here.

Author'S Note

Before you read my story, I want you to understand something.

I didn't write this book because I want anyone to feel sorry for me.

I wrote it because for a long time, I didn't understand me.

I knew what happened to me. I knew the people who hurt me. I knew the choices I made. I knew the anger, the love, the mistakes, the motherhood, the relationships, and the moments that changed my life.

What I didn't understand was how all those things connected.

For years, I thought surviving meant making it through whatever happened and continuing with life. I didn't realize survival could follow you. It could show up in the way you love, the way you argue, the way you protect yourself, the people you choose, and even the way you see yourself.

Somewhere along the way, I stopped asking only, What happened to me?

I started asking:

What did it create in me?

That's when this book became more than telling my story.

It became me looking within.

There are parts of this book that aren't pretty. There are things I'm proud of and things I'm not. There are moments when I was hurt, and there are moments when I hurt other people.

I'm not going to pretend I got everything right just because this is my story.

I didn't.

But that's part of breaking cycles too.

You have to be willing to look at what was done to you while also being brave enough to look at what you did with it.

This book doesn't contain every piece of my life. Some pieces deserve stories of their own. Some I'm still understanding. And some are still being written.

These are the pieces I'm ready to give you.

So don't read this looking for a perfect survivor.

You won't find her here.

You'll find a little girl.

A teenager.

A mother.

A woman.

A survivor.

And somewhere between all of them, you'll find me.

Trauma didn't win.

But you'll have to read the story to understand why.

— Ashari Allen

Prologue

There is a little girl sitting in the corner.

She's quiet.

Not because she doesn't have anything to say.

She's watching.

The adults are talking, moving, arguing, laughing—doing whatever adults do when they forget a child is paying attention.

But she is.

She notices everything.

The change in someone's voice.

The way a room feels before something happens.

Who is safe today.

Who isn't.

When to speak.

When to disappear.

She doesn't know words like trauma yet.

She doesn't know anxiety.

She doesn't know survival mode.

She's just a little girl.

So she watches.

And somewhere in her mind, she starts taking notes.

Remember that.

Don't do that.

Be ready.

Don't cry.

Pay attention.

She doesn't know she's building a protector.

She doesn't know some of these notes will follow her into relationships, motherhood, arguments, love, and adulthood.

She doesn't know that one day the things keeping her safe will become some of the same things she'll have to unlearn.

Right now, she's only trying to make it through childhood.

Years later, I'll spend a lot of time blaming that little girl.

I'll wonder why she's so angry.

Why she's scared to be alone.

Why she needs control.

Why she keeps showing up when I'm trying to move forward.

It'll take me years to understand something she already knew:

She wasn't trying to ruin my life.

She was trying to save it.

And one day, I'll finally sit down long enough to listen to her.

But we're not there yet.

Right now, she's still sitting in the corner.

Watching.

Learning.

Taking notes.

And this is where our story begins.
